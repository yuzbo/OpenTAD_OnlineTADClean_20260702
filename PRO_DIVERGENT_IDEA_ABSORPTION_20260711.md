## 4. Why T01 Cannot Yet Be Selected

### 4.1 The closest-prior audit missed a direct video e-process precedent

The review discussed WATCH and online video anomaly detection, but it did not include the most damaging newly found neighbor:

> Munoz, Verma, and Timans, *Detecting Object Tracking Failure via Sequential Hypothesis Testing*, WACV Workshops 2026.

That work already:

- frames a real-time video task as sequential hypothesis testing;
- uses an e-process;
- provides provable false-alert control;
- is model-agnostic and requires no retraining.

It is object-tracking failure rather than semantic action events, so it does not kill the task outright. It does kill the broad novelty claim that bringing an e-process and anytime false-alert control to a video score is itself new.

Therefore T01's Pro novelty score `22/25` and confidence `0.82` are too high.

### 4.2 Video false-alarm-rate monitoring is established adjacent territory

Online video anomaly detection already studies sequential decisions with false-alarm bounds, and surveillance evaluation commonly uses false alarms per camera hour. A paper cannot rely on the observation that clip-level error accumulates over time; that is expected repeated-testing behavior.

The contribution must instead isolate at least one nontrivial delta:

- a new valid semantic-event e-value construction under temporal dependence and nonstationarity;
- valid handling of repeated candidate spawning, resets, refractory periods, multiple classes, and multiple streams;
- a new benchmark showing that conventional evaluation selects the wrong detector for long-running use;
- or a useful risk notion that is not already covered by sequential change/anomaly monitoring.

### 4.3 The statistical object is underspecified

The review writes an update schematically as `E_t = E_{t-1}, b_t(e_t)`. The required mathematical object is multiplicative, and validity does not follow from naming a score an e-value.

At minimum the route must specify:

- the null hypothesis and its unit: frame, candidate, event-free interval, video, camera, or deployment;
- the filtration and what is predictable before each update;
- whether increments are e-values with conditional expectation at most one, or p-values transformed by a valid betting function;
- how data-dependent candidate creation and reset preserve validity;
- how temporal dependence, drift, and repeated same-class instances are treated;
- which guarantee is claimed and which assumptions make it true.

Without this, T01 risks being a conformal/e-process wrapper around an existing score.

### 4.4 The proposed risk target may be operationally wrong

`P0(ever alarm) <= alpha` over an indefinitely running stream is extremely strict. In a system expected to run for months, it can exhaust power or alpha and make the detector practically silent.

Candidate alternatives must be compared before fixing the task:

- false alarms per camera hour;
- average run length;
- event-wise false-commit risk;
- online FDR across alarms;
- renewal-window risk after a validated reset;
- cost-weighted utility under false alarm, miss, and delay costs.

The correct target depends on the application. The Pro review itself leaves false-positive, miss, and delay costs unresolved, so it cannot yet choose a universal risk definition.

### 4.5 Synthetic null streams are diagnostic, not final evidence

Block-concatenating THUMOS background clips does not produce a trustworthy real null stream:

- THUMOS annotations cover only a limited action taxonomy;
- unlabeled but semantically real actions can contaminate the null;
- clip boundaries introduce artificial distribution changes;
- concatenation does not reproduce day/night, viewpoint, environmental, and seasonal drift.

Such streams are useful for debugging and early falsification. A paper-level claim needs real long continuous negative streams or an explicitly narrower synthetic claim.

### 4.6 The proposed 100-stream calibration check is underpowered

For `alpha = 0.05`, observing 5 false alarms in 100 null streams gives approximately:

- Wilson 95% interval: `[0.0215, 0.1118]`;
- exact Clopper-Pearson 95% interval: `[0.0164, 0.1128]`.

This cannot distinguish a true error rate of 0.05 from 0.06 with useful precision. “The confidence interval contains alpha” is compatibility, not evidence that risk is controlled.

The 48-hour pilot may use 100 streams to find catastrophic failure, but any positive validity claim needs substantially more independent or block-valid replicates and a predeclared one-sided confidence criterion.

### 4.7 The first experiment must test decision value, not a mathematical truism

Showing that a repeatedly applied fixed threshold eventually fires is not enough. The pilot must show one of the following:

- current OnlineAP/latency evaluation ranks methods differently from long-horizon operational risk;
- matched-risk fixed thresholds, Bonferroni/alpha-spending, CUSUM/SPRT, WATCH-style martingales, and anomaly-FAR baselines leave a meaningful delay/power gap;
- semantic repeated-instance structure creates a problem not captured by generic change monitoring.

If none occurs, T01 should be killed even if an e-process can be attached successfully.

## 5. Reassessment of the Other Four Candidates

### 5.1 T02 Three-Clock Event Observability

This may be scientifically stronger than T01 because it introduces a CV-specific latent object:

```text
physical transition time -> first visually defensible time -> system commit time
```

FEEL 2026 makes a pilot more plausible by providing force-synchronized egocentric video, contact segmentation, and force-oriented pretraining. T02 remains conditional because:

- `tau_vis` may be observer- and instruction-dependent;
- a physical sensor transition may itself be ambiguous;
- existing latency rankings may not change after decomposition;
- the effect may hold only for a narrow contact-event subset.

Status: **co-candidate pending data and annotation audit**.

### 5.2 T03 Deadline-Censored Omission Monitoring

The task distinction is real: a missing event is not identifiable until a deadline or an irreversible impossibility state. However, PREGO/EgoProactive, procedure monitoring, temporal logic, and mistake/recovery tasks are close.

Status: **hold** until a closest-task audit proves that “earliest legal omission time” is not already represented and that natural omissions are sufficiently available.

### 5.3 P01 Risk-Constrained Camera Duty Cycling

The pre-observation distinction is valuable: saving backbone FLOPs after image capture is not equivalent to saving sensor, ISP, encode, and communication energy. But a risk guarantee may depend on minimum event duration or a sentinel that already contains the needed evidence.

Status: **hold** until hardware power decomposition and short-event miss assumptions are measured.

### 5.4 L01 Delayed-Feedback Continual Event Learning

The future-only, immutable-history protocol is conceptually clean. Its danger is benchmark fabrication: simulated delayed feedback can make almost any continual-learning method appear useful.

Status: **hold** until a realistic feedback source, chronology, delay distribution, and correction noise model are identified.

## 6. Revised Route Decision

### Adopted now

1. Freeze large PCEH/CESR formal training.
2. Retain current code only as causal infrastructure, baseline, score generator, and audit harness.
3. Keep P0 scientific repairs as infrastructure requirements: instance-aware risk sets, first-event targets, post-event masking, delayed synthetic endpoint/commit separation, and GT-taint audit.
4. Require cheap falsification before any new backbone or large visual finetuning.
5. Treat all five Pro finalists as candidates, not contributions.

### Not adopted

1. Do not declare AnytimeVideo/AVERT the final project.
2. Do not claim that semantic-video anytime validity is a new formulation merely because TAL has not used it.
3. Do not claim an infinite-horizon guarantee before defining null units, resets, dependence assumptions, and multiplicity.
4. Do not use block-concatenated THUMOS as final long-stream evidence.
5. Do not launch multi-class, multi-stream online FDR, repeated instances, localization, and a new backbone in one first paper.

## 7. Mandatory Gates Before Route Selection

### Gate N: novelty

- Compare explicitly with WACV 2026 tracking-failure e-process, WATCH, online video anomaly FAR, CUSUM/SPRT, CFAR/process control, and online multiple testing.
- State the exact delta in one sentence without relying on “first in TAL.”
- Kill T01 if the method is only score calibration plus an existing sequential test.

### Gate S: statistical validity

- Define null, filtration, predictable actions, candidate spawning, reset, repeated instances, and multiplicity.
- Select one primary risk target and one semantic event for the pilot.
- Predeclare a one-sided empirical validation criterion with adequate replication.
- Separate a diagnostic simulation claim from a real-stream validity claim.

### Gate D: data and decision value

- Obtain real long continuous negatives, or narrow the claim honestly.
- Show ranking reversal or a meaningful matched-risk delay/power gain.
- For T02, verify synchronized physical timestamps and multi-rater visual-observability intervals.
- Kill routes whose target cannot be annotated or whose metric does not change scientific conclusions.

### Gate C: cost

- First pilot must use cached/frozen scores and less than 10 GPU-hours.
- No backbone training until task value and method delta survive Gates N, S, and D.
- Continue to use full chronological evaluation even if training uses sampled episodes.

## 8. Revised 48-Hour Falsification Order

The Pro plan is retained but reordered:

1. **Hours 0-6: definition audit.** Choose one event, one null, one reset rule, one risk unit, and one application cost profile.
2. **Hours 0-12: closest-prior reproduction.** Implement or reuse fixed threshold, matched empirical threshold, Bonferroni/alpha-spending, CUSUM/SPRT, and one WATCH/e-process baseline on identical scores.
3. **Hours 6-18: null-data audit.** Mark which streams are real continuous negatives and which are synthetic diagnostics; audit unlabeled semantic events.
4. **Hours 12-30: horizon stress test.** Evaluate multiple horizons and report confidence bounds, not only point estimates.
5. **Hours 24-36: matched-risk event insertion.** Measure recall and delay at the same realized risk.
6. **Hours 30-42: T02 micro-audit.** Inspect FEEL or another synchronized source for physical/visual timestamp separation.
7. **Hours 42-48: kill decision.** Select no route unless one candidate passes novelty, validity, data, and utility gates.

## 10. Final Independent Judgment

The Pro answer is highly valuable as a **route-clearing and candidate-generation review**. Its strongest contribution is not T01 itself; it is the justified decision to stop spending compute on incremental PCEH/CESR headline claims and to move scientific validation ahead of model training.
